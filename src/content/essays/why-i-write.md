I spent most of the last decade building Penguin Eat Shabu — from one small shop to more than forty outlets across Bangkok. People assume the hard part was the food, or the rent, or the competition. It wasn't. The hard part was that almost nothing I needed to know was written down anywhere I could find it.

So I'm writing it down now.

## The gap between what gets shared and what matters

The restaurant world is loud with advice. There are courses on menu design, threads on marketing, decks on "the future of dining." Almost none of it touches the decisions that actually decided whether an outlet lived or died: how I priced a lease against realistic table turns, when to fire a manager I liked, why the fifth outlet nearly killed the company while the fortieth barely registered.

Those lessons are uncomfortable to share because they're specific, and they make me look fallible. That's exactly why they're worth sharing.
